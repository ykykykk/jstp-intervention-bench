# Joint State Transition Prediction: From Joint Change to Objects, Action, and Language

**Ke Yue (岳轲) · Research manuscript · 19 November 2026**

When a cup slips off a table, reaching for it need not require an internal movie of the falling cup. The impending movement of one's own hand can itself be the prediction. Joint State Transition Prediction (JSTP) treats a partial sensory change, an action, and a potential sequence of symbols as possible continuations of a common state, without requiring that all future variables be represented on every occasion. We distinguish this theoretical position from its more demanding claims about language-dependent judgment and self-awareness, neither of which is tested here.

We test a narrower proposition: when labels for goal-appropriate actions are scarce, can a single network reuse observed action consequences while retaining a direct route to action? Six policies were trained from scratch on the same 16,384 recorded interaction branches, with nested pools of 128, 1,024, or 16,384 goal–action annotations and three initializations. In 192 new layouts, both opposite held-out goals had to succeed after the same probe. With 128 labels, the shared-transition JSTP network attained 71.69% paired success, versus 49.79% for direct action, 67.45% for a one-step world model, and 66.75% for the matched network with consequence feedback disconnected. The last control slightly surpassed JSTP at the two larger budgets. In the scarce-label regime, replacing physically aligned latent components caused a directed action switch in 11.75% of changed-optimum trials versus 5.79% under a norm-matched null-space intervention. A separate follow-up trained nine additional dynamics models without motor gradients into dynamics and examined all seven approaches on a second set of 192 layouts; this change did not rescue the world-model comparator.

We additionally restarted two established adapters from their pinned upstream weights at each annotation budget and seed (18 new fits). Both were evaluated by actuating the original 192-layout simulator on the exact paired trials used for JSTP. With 128 annotations, official SmolVLA fine-tuning achieved 23.74% paired success and the DINOv2 / authors’ DINO-WM predictor adaptation achieved 50.58%, against 71.69% for JSTP. The paired advantages were 47.95 percentage points (97.5% layout-bootstrap interval 46.18–49.69) and 21.12 points (18.79–23.41), positive in all three matched seeds for each comparator. SmolVLA repeated the same action on 56.23% of opposite-goal pairs. DINO almost always reversed its action but often reversed in the wrong direction. Demonstration identities and update counts matched; external pretraining, optical supervision and compute did not.

These findings separate three questions: what can be done, what can be read out about co-motion, and what causally affects action within a frozen network. They support reuse of physical experience in this apparatus, not universal superiority over world models or VLAs, the discovery of full object identities, or an account of human consciousness.
