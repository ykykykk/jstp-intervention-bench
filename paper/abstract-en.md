# Same Image, Different Consequences
## Functional Grouping and Its Transfer Limits in Joint State Transition Prediction
**岳轲 (Ke Yue)**

Can a controller infer which parts move together from one reversible intervention, even when the final observation and motor history are identical? We evaluate this claim in a deterministic visual simulator with three loads and two complementary handles. Two worlds share the same current image but differ in the visual outcome of an earlier horizontal probe. A controller must first predict the consequences of two vertical pulls and then select the one that satisfies a specified goal. All models' decisions are sealed before execution. We train six learned methods under three initializations on shared observations, and include two explicit pixel-based rules. With familiar couplings and unseen two-region goals, a 36,939-parameter joint state transition predictor (JSTP) achieves 88.7% task success, versus 49.7% for the same architecture trained with independent reconstruction losses and 89.1% for a full-history direct policy. When both coupling and goal composition change, the rates are 45.8%, 50.8%, and 68.3%, respectively. Strict paired local-motion predictions are correct in 51.7% of cases for familiar couplings but only 16.0% for the held-out coupling. A three-bit rule with explicit knowledge of complementary actuation solves all four conditions. Paired consequence learning can support task-relevant grouping without object slots or segmentation labels in a familiar mechanism, but task success is not accurate local physics, and this grouping did not transfer reliably to new mechanisms.

**Keywords:** joint state transition prediction; functional grouping; visual control; paired intervention; structural generalization
